URLhaus (associated with abuse.ch) is a free Threat Intelligence source used for monitoring cyber threats
. Specifically, it provides a direct list of active malicious URLs
. A major advantage of this source is that its data can be downloaded directly as a CSV file without requiring any authentication



AbuseIPDB is a free Threat Intelligence source that provides a reliable list of reported malicious IP addresses (a blacklist)
.
Unlike URLhaus—which allows you to directly download data as a CSV—AbuseIPDB requires you to create a free account to obtain an API key
. Furthermore, when you query its API (for example, using the endpoint https://api.abuseipdb.com/api/v2/blacklist?limit=100), the data is returned in JSON format rather than a CSV file .



What are the common threat categories in the AUSIM guide?

According to your project notes based on the AUSIM/CMRPI guide, the **priority threat categories targeting Moroccan SMEs** are:

*   **Phishing, social engineering, and credential theft**.
*   **Ransomware and malware**.
*   **Web attacks and injection** (referencing the OWASP Top 10).
*   **DDoS (Distributed Denial of Service) and extortion**.

Furthermore, the actual AUSIM/CMRPI guide explicitly categorizes and defines several specific **vectors of cybercriminal attacks**, which include:

*   **Piratage and Hacking:** Exploring and exploiting security vulnerabilities, planting Trojan horses ("cheval de Troie"), and utilizing "logic bombs" to compromise systems or networks.
*   **Phishing (Hameçonnage):** Fraudulent techniques where attackers pose as trusted third parties (like banks or administrations) to deceive victims into revealing sensitive personal or financial information.
*   **Spoofing (Usurpation d'identité):** Impersonating a legitimate user or entity on a network to manipulate communications or actions.
*   **Payment Fraud (Escroquerie sur les moyens de payement):** Intercepting banking details and credentials directly from a user's computer or a merchant's client files during online transactions.
*   **Malicious Software:** The guide identifies specific threats like **Viruses** designed to cause extensive damage, **Keyloggers** that record keystrokes to steal login credentials, and **Spyware** that secretly monitors browsing habits to harvest personal data.
*   **Email-Based Threats:** This encompasses **Spam** (unsolicited mass emails used for malicious or advertising purposes), malware infections via email, and identity theft. 
*   **Financial Crimes:** The guide also highlights the risks surrounding money laundering and the misuse of virtual currencies (like Bitcoin).


Tell me more about the OWASP Top 10 categories.

In your project documents, the **OWASP Top 10** is identified as a reference framework to help establish your cybersecurity vocabulary, though you are not required to fully implement it for your data observatory. It is specifically associated with the third priority threat category targeting Moroccan SMEs: **Web and e-commerce attacks**, which involve methods like **injection** and the exploitation of **exposed data**.


The OWASP (Open Worldwide Application Security Project) Top 10 is a globally recognized standard awareness document that highlights the most critical security risks to web applications. While the list is periodically updated, the most recent categories typically include:

1.  **Broken Access Control:** Failures to properly enforce restrictions on what authenticated users are allowed to do.
2.  **Cryptographic Failures:** Previously known as Sensitive Data Exposure, this relates to failures in cryptography that lead to the compromise of sensitive data.
3.  **Injection:** Flaws (like SQL, NoSQL, or OS command injection) that occur when untrusted data is sent to an interpreter as part of a command or query.
4.  **Insecure Design:** Risks related to missing or ineffective control design in the architecture of an application.
5.  **Security Misconfiguration:** Insecure default settings, open cloud storage, or overly verbose error messages.
6.  **Vulnerable and Outdated Components:** Using components (like libraries or frameworks) with known vulnerabilities.
7.  **Identification and Authentication Failures:** Flaws that allow attackers to compromise passwords, keys, or session tokens.
8.  **Software and Data Integrity Failures:** Code and infrastructure that do not protect against integrity violations (e.g., relying on untrusted plugins or CI/CD pipelines).
9.  **Security Logging and Monitoring Failures:** Insufficient logging that allows attackers to maintain access, extract data, or destroy information without being detected.
10. **Server-Side Request Forgery (SSRF):** Flaws that occur when a web application fetches a remote resource without validating the user-supplied URL.





The primary Moroccan SME sectors you need to target are **Commerce/E-commerce**, **Banque/Finance**, and **Services** (which represent the majority of Moroccan SMEs).

Map of these threats:

*   **Commerce / E-commerce:** This sector maps directly to **Web attacks and injection** (referencing the OWASP Top 10) and **DDoS (Distributed Denial of Service) and extortion**. E-commerce platforms are prime targets for DDoS attacks, which aim to paralyze the site so attackers can demand a ransom. Additionally, this sector is highly vulnerable to **Payment Fraud** ("Escroquerie sur les moyens de payement"), where attackers intercept banking details directly from a merchant's client files during online transactions.
*   **Banque / Finance:** This sector is the primary target for **Phishing (Hameçonnage)**, **Spoofing (Usurpation d'identité)**, and **Credential Theft**. The AUSIM guide specifically defines phishing as fraudulent techniques where attackers pose as trusted institutions—like banks—to deceive victims into handing over sensitive financial information. This sector also maps to threats involving financial crimes, money laundering, and the misuse of virtual currencies.
*   **Services:** As the sector encompassing the majority of Moroccan SMEs, it is broadly vulnerable to **Ransomware, Malware, and Social Engineering**. Service SMEs heavily rely on daily operational continuity and client data; therefore, malicious software like Trojan horses ("cheval de Troie"), spyware, and ransomware are critical threats that can lock systems or steal client databases.
